Our History

The Danquah-Busia-Dombo Tradition

The New Patriotic Party (NPP) is the modern expression of one of Ghana’s oldest and most enduring political traditions — the Danquah-Busia-Dombo tradition.

This tradition began with the United Gold Coast Convention (UGCC) in 1947, founded by visionaries such as Dr. J.B. Danquah, who advocated for independence and democratic governance.

Over the decades, the tradition evolved through the United Party, the Progress Party (which produced Prime Minister Kofi Abrefa Busia), and other democratic movements that stood against military rule and one-party systems.

In 1992, with the return to constitutional democracy, the New Patriotic Party was formed. Since then, it has become one of the two dominant parties in Ghana, producing two democratically elected Presidents:

  • H.E. John Agyekum Kufuor (2001 – 2009)
  • H.E. Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo (2017 – 2025)

Today, the NPP continues to stand for democracy, the rule of law, free enterprise, and individual liberty under the banner of Development in Freedom.
